# World & Lore Bible

## The Event

No one knows what happened. One morning, everything changed.

All technology stopped working simultaneously — phones, electricity, engines, networks, all of it. Gone overnight. But that wasn't the strangest part.

The strangest part was the powers.

People woke up able to conjure fire, freeze water mid-air, call down lightning from a clear sky. Not everyone — but enough. Ordinary people: teachers, teenagers, mechanics, retirees. Most of them frightened. Some of them not.

The cause remains unknown. No warning. No explanation. No one came to explain it. The world just... changed.

---

## Tone & Atmosphere

This is **not** a classic fantasy setting. Magic is not ancient, learned, or noble here. It did not exist yesterday. The people wielding it did not study in towers or inherit power from bloodlines — they woke up with it and had to figure it out alone.

The emotional register should feel like:
- Dislocation. Power that doesn't feel like it belongs to you.
- Improvisation. People adapting to something they never asked for.
- Low-grade dread. The mystery of *why* is always present.
- Occasional dark humor. People are people even when the world breaks.

**Avoid:**
- High-fantasy clichés: "arcane", "ancient power", "the prophecy", "chosen ones"
- Magic as noble or elegant — it can be brutal, strange, uncontrolled
- Lore that implies magic was always part of this world

**Lean into:**
- Ordinary names for extraordinary things (people name their spells like they named their pets — casually, personally)
- The sense that civilization is patched together from what survived
- Enemies who are also just people/creatures who got powers — not monsters from a mythology

---

## The World Now

- Technology is gone. Civilization continues in reduced, makeshift form.
- Wizards (the common term for power-havers, though many dislike the word) are a small but significant part of the population.
- There is no governing body for magic. No schools. No guilds (yet). People learn from each other, from trial and error, from danger.
- The wilderness is more dangerous than before — creatures were affected too.

## Wizard Classes — Flavor Notes

Each class isn't a formal title — it's shorthand for what power someone manifested. They're more like descriptors than ranks.

| Class | What Happened to Them |
|---|---|
| Pyromancer | Woke up setting things on fire. Learned to aim. |
| Tidecaller | Water responds to them. Useful. Also terrifying. |
| Stonewarden | The earth listens. Slow to act, hard to stop. |
| Stormseeker | Gets static shocks constantly. Learned to redirect it. |
| Frostweaver | Everything they touch gets cold. Has its advantages. |
| Shadowblade | Their shadow does things it shouldn't. Drains to heal. |
| Dawnmage | Light bends for them. Protective. Calm. Hard to read. |
| Verdantmaker | Plants respond. Vines move. Roots obey. Unsettling. |

## Naming Conventions

Keep names grounded and functional, not mythic. Spells are named by what they do, not what legend says about them.

- Good: *Ember Shot, Rock Throw, Dark Pact, Healing Rain*
- Avoid: *Flames of Eternity, The Warden's Ancient Curse, Rite of the Frozen Tomb*

Enemy names follow the same logic — they describe what the creature is, not its place in a mythology.

## Open Questions (Intentionally Unresolved)

- What caused The Event?
- Why did only some people get powers?
- Why did technology stop?
- Are more changes coming?
- Is there a pattern to who got which type of power?

These questions are **not answered in-game**. They are the texture of the world — present in the atmosphere, never resolved.

## Lore Checklist

### Core Premise

- The world was modern until one specific day: The Event.
- Technology stopped functioning globally and suddenly.
- Magic did not exist publicly before the Event.
- Powers appeared immediately after the Event.
- The cause is unknown and remains unresolved.
- The world changed recently enough that most adults remember modern life clearly.

---

### The Event

**Tone**
- The Event should feel historical, not mythological.
- People discuss it like a real disaster or unexplained phenomenon.
- No definitive explanation exists.
- Survivors disagree on details.
- No ancient prophecy or secret magical history.

**Implementation**
- Digital records were largely lost.
- Governments collapsed unevenly.
- Eyewitness accounts contradict each other.
- Different regions use different names for the Event.
- Multiple competing theories exist.

**Avoid**
- "The chosen were awakened."
- Ancient magical civilizations.
- Gods openly revealing themselves.
- Secret wizard societies existing before the Event.

---

### Magic

**General Rules**
- Magic feels new, unstable, and improvised.
- Magic behaves more like instinct than scholarship.
- Most users learned through trial and error.
- There are patterns, but no fully understood rules.
- Emotion often influences power use.
- Magic is personal and idiosyncratic.

**Tone**
- Magic should feel strange, not elegant.
- Magic is integrated awkwardly into daily life.
- Powers are often inconvenient, embarrassing, or frightening.
- Small manifestations are common.
- Large-scale magic is rare and dangerous.

**Examples**
- Heat gathering around anxious people.
- Frost forming unconsciously during grief.
- Static electricity around certain individuals.
- Intuition becoming unnaturally accurate.
- People affecting weather, motion, or temperature subtly.

**Avoid**
- Spell circles.
- Latin incantations.
- "Mana."
- Magical energy crystals.
- Highly formalized RPG systems.
- Overly clean elemental classifications.

---

### Biology & Mutation

**Core Direction**
- Humans changed biologically after the Event.
- The changes are mostly invisible or subtle.
- Powers are tied to altered interaction with reality.
- Mutation feels adaptive, not horrific.
- Magic is partially biological and partially unexplainable.

**Implementation**
- Doctors can observe anomalies but cannot fully explain them.
- Children born after the Event may manifest differently.
- Animals and ecosystems changed too.
- Powers often correlate loosely with personality or emotional disposition.

**Avoid**
- Excessive body horror.
- Mutation monsters.
- Gross transformations.
- Superhero-style "gene activation" language.

---

### Technology

**Rules**
- Advanced technology no longer functions reliably.
- Simpler mechanical systems may still work inconsistently.
- Electricity exists, but precision systems fail.
- The world is less predictable than before.

**Worldbuilding Consequences**
- Long-distance communication is difficult.
- Transportation became localized and slower.
- Handwritten records matter again.
- Local craftsmanship became important.
- Communities depend on practical magic where possible.

**Avoid**
- Easy restoration of modern infrastructure.
- Hidden surviving super-tech societies.
- Convenient "magic batteries."
- Clean magitech aesthetics.

---

### Society

**World State**
- Civilization survived in fragmented form.
- Society is rebuilding unevenly.
- Communities are localized and practical.
- Most people are focused on survival, trade, labor, and stability.

**Magic Users**
- Power-users are uncommon but not legendary.
- Most are ordinary people.
- Society has mixed feelings toward them.
- No global magical authority exists.
- No formal magical education system exists yet.

**Social Dynamics**
- Rumors spread faster than facts.
- Regional traditions develop independently.
- People invent homemade terminology.
- Communities adapt around useful powers.

**Avoid**
- Wizard aristocracies.
- Magical empires.
- Medieval fantasy feudalism.
- "Adventurer guilds."

---

### Wilderness & Environment

**Rules**
- Nature changed subtly but meaningfully.
- Wilderness became more dangerous after the Event.
- Animals remain recognizable.
- Environmental phenomena feel uncanny rather than fantastical.

**Examples**
- Forests behaving strangely.
- Storms reacting unpredictably.
- Rivers changing course overnight.
- Animals exhibiting unnatural instincts or behaviors.

**Avoid**
- Mythological monster ecosystems.
- Fantasy biomes.
- Giant RPG creatures everywhere.

---

### Naming Conventions

**Magic**
- Names describe function, appearance, or feeling.
- Names should sound practical and human.
- Terminology evolves organically.

**Examples**
- Ember Shot, Rock Throw, Heavy Air, Flash Step, Cold Touch, Storm Pull

**Avoid**
- "Eternal Flame of the Seventh Throne"
- "Arcane Cataclysm"
- "Celestial Rite"
- "Ancient Order"

---

### Tone & Atmosphere

**Emotional Register**
- Dislocation.
- Improvisation.
- Lingering uncertainty.
- Low-grade dread.
- Occasional dark humor.
- Ordinary life continuing despite impossibility.

**Story Feel**
- The setting should feel lived-in.
- People adapt faster than they understand.
- Most conflicts are local and human.
- Mystery remains atmospheric rather than plot-driven.

**Avoid**
- Epic destiny narratives.
- Pure nihilism.
- Constant spectacle.
- Overexplaining the mystery.

---

### Open Questions (Never Fully Answered)

- What caused the Event?
- Why did technology fail?
- Why did only some people gain strong powers?
- Why do powers differ between individuals?
- Are the changes still progressing?
- Can technology ever fully return?
- Did the world become magical, or did humans become compatible with it?
- Are post-Event children changing differently?

---

### Litmus Test

When adding lore, ask:

- Does this feel like ordinary people adapting to something impossible?
- Does this avoid fantasy cliché?
- Does this preserve mystery?
- Does this feel socially and materially grounded?
- Would people realistically describe it this way?
- Does this make the world feel stranger without making it less human?
